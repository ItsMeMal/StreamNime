# StreamNime — Visual Direction

StreamNime is a content-first anime streaming interface. It should feel like a place people open to find an episode, not a company landing page.

## Visual language

- Dark cinema palette: near-black page background, charcoal surfaces, soft white text, muted cool-grey secondary text.
- One strong accent: crimson red, used for playback, active navigation, and selected states.
- Inter for headings and interface text. Typography stays compact and legible; content titles carry the hierarchy.
- Anime artwork is the main color and visual identity. Avoid decorative illustration, background patterns, artificial grain, gradient text, glass effects, and excessive motion.
- Poster shelves use consistent portrait ratios, compact metadata, and minimal framing.

## Structure

- Compact sticky header with brand, direct navigation, and an always-visible search field.
- Homepage opens with one featured anime panel, then immediately shows updated, popular, upcoming, and completed shelves.
- Catalogue pages prioritize search and a dense responsive poster grid.
- Detail pages prioritize poster, title, core metadata, synopsis, watchlist action, and the episode list.
- Watch pages prioritize a 16:9 player, source selection, title, and nearby episodes.
- Footer stays small and functional.

## Interaction and responsive behavior

- Internal navigation uses links; state changes use buttons.
- Keyboard focus is visible. Search can be focused with the slash key, except while typing in another field.
- Loading states reserve the final layout through poster skeletons. Errors provide recovery where possible.
- Desktop grids show six posters at full content width, then reduce to five, four, three, and two at narrow sizes.
- Motion is limited to short hover and loading feedback. Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Never hide critical actions behind hover. Avoid horizontal overflow and keep touch controls practical.

## Explicit exclusions

No giant marketing hero, slogans, fake social proof, investor-style sections, feature-card mosaics, unnecessary dashboards, oversized mobile posters, generic glassmorphism, decorative noise, or AI-generated filler copy.
